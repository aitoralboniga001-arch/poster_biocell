# Brainstorm: How a macrophage destroys what it engulfs

Cell Biology seminar · first-year Medicine
Team: Aitor · Nerea · Urtzi · Eneritz · Nora

---

## 1. The question behind the poster

- Engulfing is not the same as defending. Start from the alveolus and end on the lung tissue.
- One cell tells the whole story: an alveolar macrophage, shown as **successive stages in one schematic cell** (not to scale; molecular detail enlarged).
- The title says the verb: *How a macrophage **destroys** what it engulfs*.
- Running thread for the talk: from **this bacterium on the left** to **the lung tissue below**.

## 2. Layout of the printed poster

- **Two routes that converge** (the "V"):
  - **red, phagocytic route:** bacterium → nascent → early → late phagosome → phagolysosome;
  - **blue, enzyme route:** rough ER → cis-Golgi → TGN → late endosome → lysosome.
- They meet at the **phagolysosome** in the centre-bottom of the cell.
- **Numbered sections, one per speaker:**
  - 1 · Alveolar macrophage (Aitor);
  - 2 · Recognition, with 2.1–2.7 (Nerea);
  - 3 · Lysosome biogenesis, 3.1–3.6 (Urtzi);
  - 4 · Fusion, a–d (Eneritz);
  - 5 · Particles, 5.1–5.5 (Nora);
  - 6 · The conclusion line (Aitor).
- **Pathology on the right side of the same cell:** silica phagolysosome, NLRP3 inflammasome, asbestos with an open cup.
- **The lung below:** the alveolar wall and capillaries run along the bottom; on the right the interstitium thickens (inflammation, fibrosis).
- **Legend with arrow types:** phagocytic route, enzyme route, membrane fusion, recycling, pathological effect, inflammatory signal; dashed means "possible".
- **Scale references in the corner:** cell ≈ 20 µm · bacterium ≈ 2 µm · receptor ≈ 10 nm.
- **Recurring motifs:** pH labels along the red route (6.5 → 5.5 → 4.5); V-ATPases on every acidic compartment.
- References in Vancouver style along the bottom edge, numbered across all speakers.

## 4. Making it a *living poster*

The poster itself is the stage. Nothing is redrawn for the screen: the animation moves *on* the printed artwork.

- **One file, two uses:** printed for the session, and projected and animated during the talk.
- **The camera travels over the sheet:** it zooms into each section while that speaker talks, then pulls back.
- **A route map between sections:** numbered pins on each section's badge, with a traveller flying along a dotted route in the colour of the next section.
- **Talk mode and film mode:**
  - in talk mode the speaker presses → to move to the next beat, so the animation waits for us;
  - in film mode it plays on its own.
- **Beats follow the script:** each section is split into the beats of its speaker's text.
- **The scene order follows the talk:** 1 → 2 → 2.7 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6.

### Opening

- A short **wordless opener**: the title writes itself, then a dive *into* the poster.
- No narration and no captions; the speakers do the talking.

### The magnifying glass

- A brass **loupe** that magnifies the sheet, with a slow "living fluid" shimmer under the glass.
- In **scene 1** it points only at what Aitor names: the bacterium on the left, then the lung tissue below. It shows nothing inside the cell yet.
- **In the gallery** (when the poster is left on screen), visitors move the glass with the mouse. The section underneath plays in a loop inside the glass, with its name written around the rim.

### Scene animations (what moves, and why)

- **2 Recognition:** opsonins light up; receptors bind; the signalling cascade runs down to actin; the cup closes; the phagosome matures, with pH tags.
- **2.7 Killing:**
  - NOX2 subunits dock and electrons jump to make superoxide;
  - the V-ATPase spins up and the lumen glows as the pH drops (with a pH gauge);
  - hydrolases swarm and bite until the bacterium cracks and breaks apart.
- **3 Lysosome biogenesis:** we follow one hydrolase from the ER to the lysosome, while the M6P receptor takes its own recycling route back.
- **4 Fusion:**
  - a lysosome arrives; HOPS tethers it;
  - the four SNARE helices zip N → C in "bullet time" (stopwatch: ≈ milliseconds);
  - the pore opens; NSF spins the used SNAREs apart.
  - Then the fate of the products: amino acids leave through the exporter, and one rides back to the ER.
- **5 Particles:**
  - silica crystals fall with the breath, tear the phagolysosome membrane, and cathepsin B spills out;
  - NLRP3 assembles into a wheel; caspase-1 ignites; IL-1β leaves through gasdermin pores;
  - "possible lytic death" is marked *possible, not inevitable*;
  - the asbestos hood strains along the fibre three times (stopwatch: frustrated), and the fibre runs off the edge of the poster;
  - neutrophils crawl out of the capillaries; a calendar counts the years while fibrosis creeps along the wall and the breathing becomes shallow.
- **6 Conclusion:** the red and blue routes converge on the phagolysosome; the camera then goes down to the injured lung and pulls back to the whole poster.

### Instruments on screen

- A stopwatch for timescales (seconds, milliseconds, minutes to hours, "frustrated").
- A pH gauge and a calendar of years.
- Small paper tags for sizes (alveolus ≈ 200 µm, macrophage ≈ 20 µm, SNARE bundle ≈ 12 nm, GSDMD pore ≈ 20 nm, a long fibre > 15 µm).
- A microscope-objective badge that shows the current zoom (4× / 10× / 40×).

### Finale

- Out of the alveolus, back through the body, then the five signatures.

### Extras for the gallery

- **Attract mode:** with nobody at the screen, the glass wanders over the living parts of the sheet.
- **Play mode, "feed the macrophage":** throw it a bacterium, silica or asbestos, and see which one it digests, which tears its membrane and which leaves it frustrated.

## 5. Design rules we agreed on

- **The poster is the source of truth:** every animated label lights up text that is already printed.
- **"Possible" stays possible:** dashed arrows and "may" wording for outcomes that aren't certain.
- **Calm by default:** depth of field and a soft shade keep the eye on the speaker's section.
- **Smooth on a normal laptop:** a frame governor drops the costliest effects (depth-of-field blur, the glass shimmer) when the computer can't hold 60 fps.
- **Every science claim on screen is backed by the shared reference list.**
