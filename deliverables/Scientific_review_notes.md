## Details that still need confirmation, or a qualifier when presenting
1. **SNARE set.** Syntaxin-7, Vti1b and syntaxin-8 with **VAMP7** mediate late endosome–lysosome fusion. VAMP8 pairs with the same Q-SNAREs for homotypic late-endosome fusion. Applying this exact set to *phagosome*–lysosome fusion in macrophages is an extrapolation. Say "e.g." if asked; check Flannagan 2012 (ref 3).
2. **NOX2 timing.** The oxidase assembles on the phagosomal membrane as the phagosome forms. The Nunes 2013 abstract (ref 5) supports local, early regulation, but the exact wording was not confirmed in the full text.
3. **pH values** are approximate and vary with cell type and method. Published ranges are roughly early ~6.1–6.5, late ~5.5–6 and phagolysosome ~4.5–5.
4. **M6P scope.** M6P is the main route for *soluble* hydrolases. Lysosomal *membrane* proteins (including LAMPs) use cytosolic sorting motifs, and some hydrolases use LIMP-2 or sortilin (ref 6). The poster shows only the M6P route.
5. **LAMP glycocalyx as a protective barrier.** This is widely taught but partly hypothetical: LAMPs also have other roles, such as cholesterol transport (Li & Pfeffer, eLife 2016). The poster says only that heavily glycosylated LAMPs sit in the membrane.
6. **CR1 vs CR3.** CR1 binds mainly C3b/C4b (some sources also list iC3b); CR3 binds mainly iC3b.
7. **Silica → NLRP3.** The trigger (cathepsin B leak, K⁺ efflux, ROS) is still debated. The poster uses "can".
8. **Frustrated phagocytosis** depends on fibre length (roughly > 15–20 µm, with no sharp cutoff). The drawing is schematic: the fibre cannot be drawn "longer than the cell" at this scale.
9. **Alveolar macrophage location.** They sit in the alveolar lumen on the epithelium, in surfactant-rich lining fluid (ref 2). Some teaching sources say they move over the surface.
